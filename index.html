How the rig deploys

Every coin launched on Wheel gets its own fee engine, set once at mint and never changeable after. Chute runs in Parachute mode: instead of every fee locking away as static liquidity, a share of it is held back and paid out to the people still holding when price is down from its high — then rolls into permanent liquidity the moment a new high is made.

On the way down

Fees generated while price sits below its high are held in reserve and paid out to holders, in proportion to what they still hold — up to 33% of the fall covered.

On the way up

The moment price sets a new high, the reserve for that climb becomes permanent liquidity instead — locked forever, same as Standard mode.

04

Pre-flight check

What chain does Chute run on?

Robinhood Chain, launched through Wheel's factory contract. One mint transaction creates the coin, deploys its dedicated Wheel, and writes this page's link into the token so wallets and explorers can find it.

What's the difference between Standard and Parachute mode?

In Standard mode, every fee becomes locked liquidity, permanently. In Parachute mode — what Chute uses — fees generated while price is below its high are held back and paid to holders instead, covering up to 33% of a drawdown, then convert to liquidity again once a new high is set.

Is there a presale or team allocation?

No. Fixed supply of 1,000,000,000, distributed entirely through the open bonding curve from the first transaction. Nobody, including the builder, holds a pre-allocated share.
